In-Hand Salary Calculator Guide

When you receive a job offer in India, the company usually mentions the salary in terms of CTC (Cost To Company). However, the amount you actually receive in your bank account every month is called the in-hand salary or take-home salary.

Understanding the difference between CTC and in-hand salary is important before accepting a job offer. This guide explains how salary is structured and how you can estimate your real income using our calculator.

What is CTC?

CTC stands for Cost To Company. It represents the total amount a company spends on an employee in a year. It includes salary, bonuses, allowances, and benefits such as provident fund contributions.

However, not all of this money goes directly into your bank account. Some portions are deducted or allocated for benefits.

What is In-Hand Salary?

In-hand salary is the amount you actually receive every month after all deductions. It is also called the net salary or take-home salary.

The in-hand salary is usually lower than the CTC because certain deductions are applied before payment.

Common Salary Deductions in India

Several deductions reduce your take-home salary. The most common ones include:

  • Provident Fund (PF) – A retirement savings contribution.
  • Income Tax – Tax deducted based on your income slab.
  • Professional Tax – A small tax imposed by some state governments.
  • Insurance or Benefits – Health insurance or company benefits.

Old vs New Tax Regime in India

India currently has two income tax systems: the Old Tax Regime and the New Tax Regime. Taxpayers can choose the regime that results in lower tax liability.

New Tax Regime (Default System)

The new tax regime introduced under Section 115BAC offers lower tax rates but removes most deductions such as 80C, HRA, and 80D. It is now the default system for taxpayers.

For FY 2025-26, taxable income up to ₹12 lakh is effectively tax-free due to the rebate under Section 87A. Salaried employees can also claim a ₹75,000 standard deduction.

New Tax Regime Slabs (FY 2025-26)

  • ₹0 – ₹4,00,000 : 0%
  • ₹4,00,000 – ₹8,00,000 : 5%
  • ₹8,00,000 – ₹12,00,000 : 10%
  • ₹12,00,000 – ₹16,00,000 : 15%
  • ₹16,00,000 – ₹20,00,000 : 20%
  • ₹20,00,000 – ₹24,00,000 : 25%
  • Above ₹24,00,000 : 30%

Old Tax Regime

The old tax regime allows taxpayers to reduce their taxable income using deductions and exemptions. Common deductions include:

  • Section 80C investments (up to ₹1.5 lakh)
  • Health insurance under Section 80D
  • House Rent Allowance (HRA)
  • Home loan interest

Salaried individuals can also claim a ₹50,000 standard deduction under the old regime.

Old Tax Regime Slabs

  • ₹0 – ₹2,50,000 : 0%
  • ₹2,50,000 – ₹5,00,000 : 5%
  • ₹5,00,000 – ₹10,00,000 : 20%
  • Above ₹10,00,000 : 30%

Taxpayers with high deductions often benefit more from the old regime, while those with fewer investments usually pay less tax under the new regime.

Example: CTC vs In-Hand Salary

Suppose your CTC is ₹10,00,000 per year. After deductions such as PF and taxes, your actual in-hand salary might be around ₹65,000 to ₹75,000 per month depending on your tax bracket and company structure.

How to Calculate In-Hand Salary

The basic process involves:

  1. Start with your annual CTC.
  2. Subtract provident fund contributions.
  3. Subtract income tax deductions.
  4. Divide the remaining amount by 12 to get monthly salary.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is CTC the same as in-hand salary?

No. CTC includes benefits and employer contributions, while in-hand salary is the amount you actually receive after deductions.

Why is my in-hand salary lower than my CTC?

Because deductions such as provident fund, income tax, and professional tax reduce the amount paid to you each month.

Which tax regime is better?

It depends on your income and deductions. If you invest heavily in tax-saving instruments, the old regime may be better. Otherwise, the new regime often results in lower tax.
