CAGR Guide

Understand Compound Annual Growth Rate and how investors measure long-term returns.

What is CAGR?

CAGR stands for Compound Annual Growth Rate. It measures the average yearly growth of an investment over a specific period of time assuming that profits are reinvested every year.

Investors use CAGR because it gives a smoother and more realistic picture of growth compared to simple average returns.

Why CAGR is Important

Financial markets rarely grow at the same rate every year. Some years may deliver high returns while others may see losses. CAGR simplifies these fluctuations and shows the effective annual growth rate.

  • Shows true investment growth
  • Useful for comparing multiple investments
  • Removes volatility from yearly returns
  • Widely used in finance and business analysis

CAGR Formula

The CAGR formula is:

CAGR = (Final Value / Initial Value)1 / Years − 1

Where:

  • Final Value = Value of investment at the end
  • Initial Value = Starting investment amount
  • Years = Investment duration

CAGR Example

Suppose you invest ₹1,00,000 in a mutual fund and after 5 years the value becomes ₹1,80,000.

Using the formula:

CAGR = (180000 / 100000)1/5 − 1

The CAGR comes out to approximately 12.47% per year.

This means your investment effectively grew at around 12.47% annually.

Where CAGR is Used

  • Stock market analysis
  • Mutual fund performance comparison
  • Startup growth evaluation
  • Business revenue growth analysis
  • Long-term investment planning

CAGR vs Absolute Return

Absolute return only measures total gain over a period, while CAGR shows average yearly growth.

  • Absolute Return: Total percentage gain
  • CAGR: Annualized growth rate

Because of this, CAGR is more useful when comparing investments of different time durations.
